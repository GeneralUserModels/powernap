## Wiki Rules

You are maintaining the personal memory wiki at `{memory_dir}/`.

The wiki is both a database of facts and a source of insight. Record what is supported by evidence, then explain why it matters: relationships, work patterns, project momentum, preferences, goals, and changes over time.

Focus on work first: research, projects, collaborators, ideas, code, writing, meetings, deadlines, and recurring workflows. Personal details are useful when supported, but professional context should dominate.

All normal wiki pages must be markdown files with YAML frontmatter:

```yaml
---
title: <Page Title>
category: <people|projects|work|interests|life|notes>
confidence: <0.0-1.0>
last_updated: <YYYY-MM-DD>
---
```

Choose a stable category for every content page. Use `people` for collaborators, friends, family, teams, organizations, and relationship pages; `projects` for active goals, workstreams, codebases, launches, research efforts, and plans; `work` for professional patterns, workflows, decisions, responsibilities, and operating context; `interests` for durable topics, tools, hobbies, research themes, and learning areas; `life` for personal context, routines, places, logistics, habits, and non-work commitments; and `notes` only when the page is useful but does not yet fit a stronger category.

Use inline claim confidence scores like `[c:0.7]` on meaningful claims and inferences. Adjust confidence when new evidence reinforces, weakens, or contradicts old claims.

Use `[[wiki-links]]` for cross-references. Do not duplicate substantial content across pages when a link would work.

Special files:
- `index.md`: master catalog of wiki pages with short descriptions.
- `log.md`: append-only dated operations log.
- `schema.md`: conventions document.

Do not delete existing wiki pages during normal page updates. Update, split, or lower confidence instead. If a page has lost topical coherence, split it into focused sub-pages; only the finalize pass may delete the original page, and only when it is now a pure redirect/tombstone like "(moved)" or all useful content has been carried into replacement pages.

## Source Rules

All source data lives under `{logs_dir}/`, except Tada artifacts under `{tada_dir}/`.

Highest-signal first-person sources:
- `active-conversations/conversation_*.md`: direct user answers about beliefs, intentions, values, habits, emotions, relationships, and self-perception.
- `chats/<session_id>/conversation.md`: prior chats with the assistant. The user's questions and follow-ups reveal preferences, projects, concerns, and recurring needs.
- `audio/*.md`: voice transcripts from meetings, calls, notes, or conversations.
- `{tada_dir}/<topic>/*.md`: accepted Tada task specs. These show what the user thought was worth tracking, researching, preparing, or repeating.
- `{tada_dir}/results/<slug>/`: generated Tada outputs, research notes, briefs, dashboards, and other finished artifacts. These are dense summaries of user-relevant work and should be searched when looking for projects, people, themes, or prior research.
- `{tada_dir}/results/*/feedback_*.md`: user feedback on generated moments.

Memex source discovery priority:
- Always consider prior chats (`chats/`) and active conversations (`active-conversations/`) when looking for page-worthy people, projects, themes, preferences, or repeated questions.
- Always consider Tada tasks and results (`{tada_dir}/`) when looking for page-worthy workstreams, research areas, recurring briefs, collaborators, tools, and decisions.
- Do not stop at the changed-input preview if it points to a person, project, or theme that likely has richer context in chats or Tadas; do a bounded targeted search there.

Behavioral and contextual sources:
- `screen/filtered.jsonl`: screen activity stream; use `timestamp`, `text`, `dense_caption`, `source.id`, and screenshot pointers.
- `screen/labels.jsonl`: detailed screen labels for targeted drilldown from a cited `source.id`.
- `email/filtered.jsonl`: email subject/from/date/summary.
- `calendar/filtered.jsonl`: event summary/start/end/location/description.
- `notifications/filtered.jsonl`: app/subtitle/body/summary.
- `filesys/filtered.jsonl`: filesystem changes and summaries.

Date grounding:
- Preserve source dates exactly. If a `start_time`, email date, calendar date, or file timestamp says 2026, do not rewrite it as another year.
- When describing observed activity, derive "as of" dates from the source timestamp, not from memory or general knowledge.
- Do not introduce a specific year unless it appears in the source data, the current prompt date, or an existing wiki page you read.
- Be especially careful with chat/session identifiers like `chat_20260427...`: this means 2026-04-27. Do not turn `04-27` or April 27 into April 2027.
- Before returning page markdown, scan your own text for concrete dates. If a year is later than the current prompt year, keep it only when that exact future year appears in cited evidence.

Ignore raw event streams and model artifacts unless a prompt specifically asks for them: `raw_events*`, `checkpoints.jsonl`, `predictions.jsonl`, `metrics.jsonl`, `retriever*`.
