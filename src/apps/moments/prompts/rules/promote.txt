## Rules

- Promote only candidates that are clearly useful and executable.
- Rank the best candidates first. A top-ranked candidate must have a concrete `likely_next_need`, specific sources, a clear user benefit, and a concrete artifact that advances the user's future work rather than merely describing past activity.
- Prefer candidates that extend the user's current trajectory through added research, synthesis, experimentation, preparation, contextualization, or decision support.
- Do not over-privilege professional or technical work. Rank near-term real-world commitments higher when the artifact prevents dropped balls, reduces coordination friction, or helps the user show up prepared for people, places, or events.
- Keep lower-priority but still viable candidates in `ranked`; Python applies the top-K cutoff.

## Tips

- Python deterministically routes candidates whose slug matches an existing accepted moment as updates to that moment.
- Promote an update candidate only when its new evidence, scope, instructions, or artifact would make the accepted moment better.
- Reject near-duplicates that use a different slug from an accepted moment unless they are meaningfully distinct.
- Down-rank or reject candidates whose `likely_next_need` is vague, generic, or disconnected from the requested artifact.
- Down-rank passive recaps unless the artifact clearly enables a future action, decision, or workflow.
- Down-rank static inventories unless they include prioritization, implications, recommended actions, or a decision frame tied to the likely next need.
- Do not call tools. Do not read files, run shell commands, or write accepted markdown. Python will persist top-ranked moments after parsing your JSON.
